# Freeze Sprint — Part 3: Targeted Benchmark Expansion Design

## Untested anchor families (from the calibration rerun)

1. Enterprise SaaS customer growth
2. Consumer customer growth
3. Marketplace growth (two-sided)
4. Commerce/DTC growth
5. Insurance growth (policy/customer count — distinct from Oscar Health, which had loss data but no growth series)
6. Hardware growth
7. Deeptech/partnership growth
8. Non-SaaS Unit Economics families (marketplace, hardware, commerce/DTC, deeptech; insurance partially tested via Oscar Health but still provisional)
9. Growth Velocity at a materially different starting scale than Shopify's (need something much larger or much smaller, above-floor)
10. Revenue Growth at any scale (currently ZERO real examples anywhere in the 15-company portfolio — the single most urgent gap)

## Scope decision for this turn

Building all 9 with the same sourcing discipline as the original 20-company portfolio (dated
primary sources, evidence-repair-level verification, explicit exclusion of unverifiable claims) is
a substantial research undertaking. Rather than rush 9 companies through shallow, under-verified
sourcing — which would violate this program's own standing discipline ("historical integrity over
completeness," "do not manufacture") — **this turn executes real, sourced research for 4 companies
(#1-4 above)**, chosen because each maps to a *currently completely untested* family and together
they cover Commerce/DTC, Insurance, Marketplace, and Hardware growth plus three non-SaaS Unit
Economics families.

**Explicitly deferred, not fabricated:** Enterprise SaaS growth, Consumer growth, Deeptech/
partnership growth, and Revenue-Growth-at-large-scale remain untested after this turn. This is
reported honestly in Part 6/8 as a real, named scope limitation — not glossed over, and no evidence
is invented to paper over it.

## Selection discipline

Every company below was chosen for its **business-model/anchor-family fit**, decided before any
evidence was pulled — not for a desired score or tier outcome. The anti-contamination protocol
(Part 4) governs execution: a historical cutoff date is fixed for each company *before* any
evidence beyond the basic "did this company exist and roughly when" is gathered, and no source
dated after that cutoff is used for scoring.
